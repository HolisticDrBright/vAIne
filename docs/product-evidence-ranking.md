# Product evidence ranking

## Purpose

vAIne ranks eligible products by evidence for the appearance goals identified in a check-in. Most scores are ingredient-evidence scores, not claims that a finished product was clinically proven. A small, explicit product-specific review layer is used only where source, characterization, or finished-product human evidence has been reviewed. Product marketing, affiliate status, commission, and price never increase an evidence score.

## Order of operations

1. Apply allergy, pregnancy or nursing, recent-procedure, sensitivity, fragrance, and duplicate-active exclusions.
2. Keep products that match an appearance goal from the check-in.
3. Score the ingredients for those matched goals using the evidence rules below.
4. Apply the selected price ceiling.
5. Rank by evidence score and breadth of matched goals. Product-specific floors are conservative and apply only to the reviewed product and relevant goals.
6. Break an otherwise equal tie by lower price for a capped budget or higher price when the user selected no price limit.

Rinse-off cleansers receive a contact-time adjustment. A missing concentration, delivery-system detail, or product-specific trial prevents the ingredient score from being presented as proof of finished-product efficacy.

## Routine-size selection

The person chooses a maximum of 2, 4, 6, or 8 unique products across the complete morning, evening, and weekly plan. The builder prioritizes protection and the strongest goal-matched treatment first, then hydration and cleansing, followed by distinct peptide, EV/conditioned-media, toner/essence, and weekly roles as the selected size expands. One product used morning and evening counts once. Missing safe matches produce category guidance rather than padding the plan with a weak or duplicate product.

Weekly masks are selected as a separate role when they match the visible goals and sensitivity answers. This schedule priority is not an inflated efficacy score. Clay masks and physical/acid scrubs are never treated as daily cleansers and are not placed on the same night as retinoids, other exfoliating acids, microneedling, or another intensive treatment.

## Advanced peptide and EV review

Exosome count and price are not valid stand-alone quality measures. The review compares disclosed biological source, particle identity and abundance methods, purity/contamination controls, stability, and finished-product human evidence. MISEV2023 recommends source-specific reporting, orthogonal characterization, particle abundance, EV-associated components, and assessment of non-vesicular co-isolates; products do not become directly comparable merely by publishing a particle number.

## Current catalog limitations

The catalog contains 122 entries. The source audit found 111 research-only entries, seven blocked entries, four out-of-scope entries, and 57 entries with unknown skin-type compatibility. The imported workbook rows do not yet contain structured product-study fields. The separate reviewed registry in `src/data/advancedProductEvidence.ts` captures the narrow product-specific review above without weakening the workbook's catalog and safety gates.

Before paid launch, each visible product needs current official product-page verification, complete INCI and active concentration where available, a skin-type and irritation review, sunscreen Drug Facts where applicable, and an independent reviewer decision. Product-specific trials should be linked separately and should supersede ingredient-only evidence only after study-quality and conflict-of-interest review.
